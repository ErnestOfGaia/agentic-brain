# Welcome to AI Coaching

Your organization already has AI embedded in your daily tools. Microsoft Copilot, Zoom, email, and documents now include AI capabilities whether you're actively using them or not. This coaching is designed to help you understand what you have, how to use it responsibly, and where AI can reduce friction in your actual work.

## What We'll Cover in Our First Session

* Understanding AI Fundamentals — what AI actually does, what it can't do, and why the distinction matters for your work

* Understanding AI Within Tools and As Tools — recognizing AI features you already have, and evaluating new AI tools against your business needs

* Understanding Cloud and Local AI — how deployment choices affect cost, control, privacy, and performance for your organization

---

Bring your questions and the specific tools you use most. We'll focus on practical applications that fit your team's workflow and budget.

---

## What I Need to Know

Before we meet, please think through these questions. Your answers help me tailor the first session to what matters most for your role and your team. You don't need to write formal responses—just be honest about where you stand.

1. **What tools does your team use daily?** (e.g., Microsoft 365, Zoom, Slack, Google Workspace, industry-specific software) — This tells me what AI capabilities you already have access to.

2. **Have you noticed AI features in any of your existing tools, or is this the first time you're thinking about it?** — This helps me know whether to start with "here's what you already have" or "here's what's available."

3. **What's one specific task or workflow in your role that feels repetitive or time-consuming?** — This is where we often find the quickest wins with AI.

4. **Does your business rely on your web presence (website, social media, email marketing, online reputation)?** — This shapes whether we prioritize content creation, SEO, or customer-facing AI tools.

5. **What concerns do you have about using AI?** (e.g., cost, security, replacing people, quality, privacy) — I want to address what's actually on your mind, not dismiss your concerns.

6. **Do you have budget flexibility to test new AI tools, or do you need to work primarily with what you already have?** — This determines whether we explore new options or maximize existing ones.

7. **Who else on your team should eventually understand this stuff, and what's their comfort level with technology?** — This helps me understand if we're coaching just you or if we're building team knowledge.

8. **What would success look like for you three months from now?** (e.g., saving time, making better decisions, producing better work, understanding your options) — This gives me a target to work toward.

9. **Is there an AI topic or use case you've been curious about but didn't know who to ask?** — Sometimes the best starting point is the question that's been nagging at you.

---
**These questions are designed to:**

- **Assess current state** — what tools they have, what they know, what concerns them
- **Identify quick wins** — the repetitive task or workflow where AI could help immediately
- **Understand constraints** — budget, team scope, business model (web-dependent vs. not)
- **Surface real objections** — cost, security, job displacement—the things actually keeping them awake
- **Define success** — what does "better" look like for them in 3 months
- **Uncover latent curiosity** — the question they've been afraid to ask

Each question includes a brief note explaining _why_ you're asking it, so if you adapt this for different contexts (email, landing page, etc.), you understand the purpose behind each one.
